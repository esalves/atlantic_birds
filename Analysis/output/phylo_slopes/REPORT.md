# Phylogenetic species slopes: does the year-slope itself carry a Brownian signal?

Generated by `Analysis/scripts/atlantic_phylo_slopes.R --trees 50` (run 2026-09-22, glmmTMB
1.1.14). Full log: `phylo_slopes_50trees.log` (session scratchpad). Results:
`Analysis/output/phylo_slopes/phylo_slopes_results.rds`.

Gustavo's comment: the response to change (the year slope) can itself be an extended
phenotype, so species slopes should get a phylogenetic (Brownian) structure, not just
species intercepts. This adds a second `propto()` covariance block whose design columns
are `species indicator x scaled_yr`, with a copy of each tree's correlation matrix `A`
relabelled to match (`Asl`; see the script header for the exact code and why
`_phylo_engine.R` was not touched -- it is sourced concurrently by other scripts).

**No response shows evidence of a phylogenetic slope.** LRT statistics sit well below the
1-df boundary-corrected null in every case (0/50 trees significant at p < 0.05 for any
response), and AIC worsens by ~1.7-2 units on adding the phylo-slope variance component
(the expected cost of an uninformative extra parameter). H2_slope is small (3% wing M0,
0.6% wing M3, 2.7% isometry) except ln body mass (12%, still with a wide/uninformative
fixed-effect CI). The fixed `scaled_yr` estimate itself is essentially unchanged between
P0 and P1 in every response (largest shift: wing M0, -0.922 -> -0.866, still overlapping).

## 5. Interpretation

Adding a phylogenetic (Brownian) structure to the species-specific year slopes, alongside
the existing phylogenetic species intercept, makes essentially no difference to this
dataset: the phylogenetic proportion of slope variance (H2_slope) is 0.6% for the fully
adjusted wing model (M3) and 3.2% for the unadjusted baseline (M0), an order of magnitude
smaller than the phylogenetic proportion already carried by the intercept (~94-96%,
`controlled_wing_phylo_results.rds`). The REML likelihood-ratio test against the
boundary-corrected null finds no tree, of 50, where the phylogenetic slope improves the
fit at p < 0.05, and AIC consistently favours dropping it. The controlled year effect
(M3) itself is unchanged to two decimals whether or not the slope carries phylogenetic
structure (-0.371 vs -0.364 SD-year^-1), and the resulting species-level slopes correlate
at r = 0.9999 with the published (intercept-only-phylogenetic) values, so neither the
headline estimate nor the caterpillar figure would change under this extension. The one
partial exception is ln body mass, where H2_slope reaches 12% [9-15%] -- still modest, and
the fixed year effect for mass is essentially flat and non-significant regardless (P0
-0.48%/decade, P1 -0.38%/decade), so this is not evidence of a phenotypically meaningful
phylogenetic slope in mass either. glmmTMB's `propto` cannot fit a *correlated*
intercept-slope phylogenetic block (a Sigma-kron-A structure), only two independent
Brownian terms as fit here; a correlated version -- which would let a species' ancestral
state and its rate of change covary phylogenetically, the more standard "evolutionary
rate" formulation -- would need Bayesian brms/Stan and is not attempted here.

## 6. Draft manuscript text

**Methods (replaces `Manuscript/index.qmd` ~line 486, the sentence "The phylogenetic
covariance applies to the species intercepts only... independent among species rather
than a Brownian process."):**

> The phylogenetic covariance applies to the species intercepts; species year slopes
> come from the non-phylogenetic `(1 + scaled_yr || Binomial)` term and are independent
> among species rather than a Brownian process. We tested whether the year slopes
> themselves carry a phylogenetic signal by adding a second phylogenetic covariance term
> on the species x year design columns (glmmTMB's `propto` structure; Williams et al.
> 2025), alongside the existing phylogenetic species intercept and the non-phylogenetic
> slope, across the same 50 phylogenies used throughout. This partitions the species
> slope variance into a phylogenetic and an independent component
> (H2_slope = sigma2_phylo / (sigma2_phylo + sigma2_iid)); a REML likelihood-ratio test
> against the 50:50 chi-bar-squared boundary null (Self & Liang 1987) evaluated whether
> the added term improved the fit.

**Results (2-3 draft sentences):**

> Adding a phylogenetic structure to the species-specific year slopes did not improve
> the fit of either the baseline (M0) or the fully adjusted (M3) wing model: the
> phylogenetic proportion of slope variance was small in both (H2_slope = 3.2% and 0.6%
> respectively, an order of magnitude below the ~95% phylogenetic proportion of the
> species intercept), and a REML likelihood-ratio test against the boundary-corrected
> null found no evidence for the added term on any of the 50 trees (mean AIC penalty
> +1.7 to +2.0 units for the extra parameter). The controlled year effect and the
> species-level slopes were essentially unchanged whether or not the slope carried
> phylogenetic structure (M3: -0.371 vs -0.364 SD-year^-1 per decade of scaled year;
> species slopes r = 0.9999), so we retain the independent-slope specification reported
> throughout.
